You don't need to master AI to get real value from it. You need a few prompts that actually work and a minute to try them.

Here are five I use. Each one solves a real problem that used to take longer than it should.

## 1. Turn a messy email thread into a clear summary

You know that email chain that's 22 replies deep and you need to figure out what was actually decided? Paste the whole thing into ChatGPT and say:

> "Summarize this email thread in bullet points. Pull out any decisions made, action items, and who's responsible for each."

What used to take 15 minutes of scrolling takes about 10 seconds.

## 2. Write a first draft of anything

Blank page, blinking cursor, no idea where to start. This works every time:

> "Write a first draft of a [document type] for [audience]. The main point I want to make is [your point]. Keep it [tone]."

You're not handing your work over. You're skipping the hardest part — getting something on the page — and editing from there instead of starting from nothing.

## 3. Prep for a meeting in three minutes

Before anything important, try this:

> "I have a meeting with [person or role] about [topic]. What are three questions I should ask? What should I be ready to explain?"

Two minutes of typing. You walk in sharper than you would have otherwise.

## 4. Fix something that hits the wrong tone

You wrote something and it sounds too stiff. Or too casual. The content is right, the tone is off:

> "Rewrite this to sound more [tone — friendly, professional, direct, warm]. Keep all the same information. Just change how it reads. Here's the original: [your text]"

This is one of the most underrated uses of AI. Adjusting tone is tedious to do manually. It takes seconds here.

## 5. Make sense of something confusing

You got a document, a contract, a technical explanation, and you're not sure what it actually means or what you're supposed to do with it:

> "Explain this to me like I'm not technical. What does it actually mean and what should I do about it? Here's the text: [paste it]"

Stops you from either ignoring confusing things or spending 30 minutes trying to decode them yourself.
