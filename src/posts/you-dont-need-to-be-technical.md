Here's the thing I hear most from people who haven't started using AI yet: "I'm not really a tech person."

I get why it feels that way. Most of the coverage is full of engineers talking about models, parameters, and training data. If you're not in that world, it's easy to assume the tools are for them and not for you.

They're not.

## You don't need to understand it to use it

You don't know how a microwave works. You still heat up your lunch. You don't know how GPS calculates a route. You still get where you're going.

AI tools work the same way. You don't need to know what's happening under the hood. You just need to know what to type.

## Three things you can try today

**Drafting emails.** If you ever stare at a blank reply and can't find the words, paste the original email into ChatGPT and say: "Help me write a professional response to this. The main thing I want to say is [your point]." You'll have a draft in seconds. Edit it, send it.

**Summarizing documents.** Got a long report, a contract, or an article you need to get through? Paste it in and say: "Summarize this in plain language. What are the key points and anything I need to act on?" Instead of 20 minutes of reading, you get the important parts in under a minute.

**Thinking things through.** This one surprises people. AI isn't just for writing. You can describe a decision you're trying to make and ask: "What am I not thinking about here? What factors should I consider?" It surfaces angles you might have missed — like having a smart friend to think out loud with.

## You can't break it

There's no wrong way to start. If you type something and the answer isn't quite right, say so. "Make it shorter." "Sound less formal." "Actually, I meant something more like this." AI responds to feedback, and each refinement gets you closer to what you need.

The only thing that improves your results is practice. Not studying. Not a technical background. Just using it a few times a week until it feels normal.

Try one of those three things before the end of the week. Just pick the one that fits something you're already doing. That's the whole assignment.
